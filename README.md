### 🔬 // RESEARCH // PEER_REVIEWED

**AI-Powered Dental Prosthetic and Recommendation System** · Paper `FPC10` · **Accepted — oral presentation**
*ESOFT International Conference 2026 (EICON 2026), ESOFT University, Kandy* · presented 30 Aug 2026

<div align="left">
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/STATUS-ACCEPTED-FF0000?style=flat-square&labelColor=0A0A0A" alt="Status: accepted" />
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/PEER_REVIEW-2_REVIEWERS-FF0000?style=flat-square&labelColor=0A0A0A" alt="Peer review: 2 reviewers" />
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/RADIOGRAPHS-16%2C995-FF0000?style=flat-square&labelColor=0A0A0A" alt="Radiographs: 16,995" />
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/CLASSES-10-FF0000?style=flat-square&labelColor=0A0A0A" alt="Condition classes: 10" />
  <img src="https://img.shields.io/badge/mAP%400.5-0.581-FF0000?style=flat-square&labelColor=0A0A0A" alt="mAP at 0.5: 0.581" />
</div>

A fine-tuned **YOLOv8-Nano** detector reads ten classes of dental condition from panoramic radiographs; a hybrid rule-based scoring engine over a nine-treatment knowledge base ranks candidate prosthetic treatments by clinical, demographic and budget factors, and returns the top five with rationales, indicative costs and a downloadable clinical-handover report.

Commercial dental AI reads radiographs and marks pathology — it doesn't say what to do about it for *this* patient. The contribution is the integration: every ranked recommendation traces back to the finding that triggered it. Sole author-developer; both co-authors supervised the project. The headline figure above is the **held-out test split**, not the validation split the manuscript reports — and the [case study](https://akila-portfolio.vercel.app/work/dental-ai-pro) publishes the per-class table where that average hides the truth.

### 🧭 // HOW_I_WORK

- I'd rather ask one clarifying question early than rebuild a week later.
- I write the trade-off down. If I chose something, I can tell you what I rejected and why.
- I read the existing code before adding to it, and match its idiom rather than importing my own.
